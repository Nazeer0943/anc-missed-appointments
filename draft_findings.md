# ANC Appointment Analysis: Findings

## Overall appointment outcomes

The analysis included 1,800 valid records. 1,092 (60.67%) were classified as missed appointments, while 708 (39.33%) were not missed.

## Comparisons across selected characteristics

- For **residence**, the highest observed missed-appointment rate was 64.17% in the group 'Rural', compared with the lowest rate of 56.51% in 'Urban'.
- For **education level**, the highest observed missed-appointment rate was 63.66% in the group 'Primary', compared with the lowest rate of 58.10% in 'No formal education'.
- For **employment status**, the highest observed missed-appointment rate was 65.52% in the group 'Student', compared with the lowest rate of 57.25% in 'Unemployed'.
- For **parity**, the highest observed missed-appointment rate was 62.20% in the group '4', compared with the lowest rate of 55.24% in '5'.
- For **health insurance**, the highest observed missed-appointment rate was 62.55% in the group 'No', compared with the lowest rate of 55.27% in 'Yes'.
- For **partner support**, the highest observed missed-appointment rate was 64.19% in the group 'Low/None', compared with the lowest rate of 56.99% in 'High'.
- For **phone access**, the highest observed missed-appointment rate was 74.45% in the group 'No reliable phone', compared with the lowest rate of 58.70% in 'Shared phone'.
- For **appointment type**, the highest observed missed-appointment rate was 61.70% in the group 'Follow-up', compared with the lowest rate of 55.48% in 'High-risk review'.
- For **barrier reported at booking**, the highest observed missed-appointment rate was 64.83% in the group 'Long waiting time', compared with the lowest rate of 58.97% in 'Feeling unwell'.
- For **previous missed appointments**, the highest observed missed-appointment rate was 79.31% in the group '3', compared with the lowest rate of 55.61% in '0'.

## Interpretation and limitations

These comparisons describe differences in the observed missed-appointment rates. They do not, by themselves, establish that any characteristic causes missed appointments. Further statistical testing and consideration of the dataset's source and limitations are needed before drawing stronger conclusions.
